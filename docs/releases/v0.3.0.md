DiskPilot 0.3.0 can empty the Trash for you, and shows exactly what it is removing while it does.

## Download

**[DiskPilot.dmg](https://github.com/Devanshgoel-123/diskpilot/releases/download/v0.3.0/DiskPilot.dmg)**. One universal app for Apple silicon and Intel Macs, macOS 11 or later.

To update, quit DiskPilot, then drag the new version into Applications and replace the old one. Your history, settings and saved results are kept. If macOS says it cannot verify the developer, open **System Settings > Privacy & Security** and select **Open Anyway** once.

## What's new

### Empty Trash

- **Empty Trash** is on the Cleanup page and shows how much space the Trash is using. It is also offered right after you move items to the Trash.
- Before anything is deleted you see the space you will get back, the number of files, and the items themselves, largest first. Nothing happens until you choose **Empty Trash**.
- Only the items in that preview are deleted. Anything you move to the Trash afterwards is left alone, and an item that is no longer there is skipped.
- Progress is real: the amount reclaimed and the file count come from the deletion as it happens. **Stop** finishes the current file and leaves everything else in the Trash.
- When it is done you see the space reclaimed and files removed. If some items could not be removed, for example locked files or files owned by another user, each one is listed with the reason and stays in the Trash.
- Every run is recorded in Cleanup History.

macOS keeps the Trash private. To empty it from DiskPilot, turn on DiskPilot in **System Settings > Privacy & Security > Full Disk Access**. DiskPilot explains this and opens the right page if access is missing.

Deletion never follows links out of the Trash and never crosses onto another volume. The Trash on external drives is not included.

Everything still runs locally, with no account and no telemetry.
